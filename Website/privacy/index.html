What the app stores, and where

  • Your notes are the Markdown files in your own folders. Notebook reads and writes them in place. Pictures and files you add to a note go into a hidden .assets folder next to it.
  • The folders you add as locations are remembered on your device, with the permission to open them again. On iPhone and iPad, notes you keep on the device are in Notebook's own folder, which the Files app shows under On My iPhone › Notebook.
  • Favourites, recently opened files, the folders open in the sidebar and your settings (typeface, text size, what the sidebar shows, the notebook on the app icon) stay in the app's preferences on your device.
  • The folders you hide and the reminders you set are kept in the app's preferences and in iCloud's key-value store, which Apple syncs to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. A reminder is the place of the note in your folders, its time and the place it reminds you at (a name and its coordinates); it is never written into the note, so it doesn't go along when you share the note.
  • Earlier versions of your notes, from version 1.1, for version history: plain copies of a note's text, taken as you write and kept for a year. Each copy's name says when it was taken and on what kind of device, with a few letters that tell your devices apart. The versions of notes in iCloud are kept in Notebook's own iCloud folder, which Apple syncs to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, as it syncs iCloud Drive; the folder doesn't show in iCloud Drive. The versions of notes anywhere else stay in the app's own folder on that device. Settings › your name › iCloud › Storage on iPhone and iPad, or System Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage on the Mac, shows how much Notebook keeps in iCloud, and can delete it.
  • A to-do's date, time and place are part of the to-do: they are written into the note after its words, a place as a link to it in Apple Maps, so they go along with the note like everything else in it.
  • To-dos you add in To-dos without a note, from version 1.1, are a plain Markdown checklist, To-dos.md, in Notebook's own iCloud folder, which Apple syncs to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, as it syncs the earlier versions of notes; the folder doesn't show in iCloud Drive. Without iCloud it stays in the app's own folder on that device. Moving such a to-do into a note writes it into that note.
  • The places you chose last, to choose again at a tap, are kept in the app's preferences on that device.
  • Home and Work, once you choose them for reminders, are kept in the app's preferences and, in the App Store build, in iCloud's key-value store, for your other devices: each an address and its coordinates. Choosing one from Contacts opens Apple's own contact picker; Notebook sees only the address you tap and has no access to your contacts.
  • What you share to Notebook from another app's share sheet (text, a link, pictures, files) waits in a folder that Notebook and its share extension share on your device, until Notebook has written it into your notes, and is then deleted. For the share sheet to offer them, the name of your default folder and the place of the note you opened last are kept there too.
  • What the widgets show: the to-dos due today, overdue or in the coming week, with the names of their notes, and the text of the notes you chose to show in a Note widget, kept by Notebook in a folder it shares with its widgets on your device, and a list of up to 500 of your notes' names and folders to choose from, none from folders you hid. A to-do you tick in the widget waits there, with the reminders it took back, until Notebook has written the tick into its note. It never leaves the device.
  • The search index of file names, the text of notes and PDFs, and the words Notebook reads in pictures, scanned pages and handwriting is kept in the app's caches on each device, so search is ready the moment the app opens. Each device builds its own from your files, and it never leaves the device.
  • Spotlight gets the name, the first lines, the folder and the text of every note in your locations, except those in hidden folders, so the system's search finds them. Spotlight keeps its index on your Mac, iPhone or iPad, as it does for Mail or Notes, and it never leaves the device. Notebook keeps a list of the notes it gave Spotlight, by their places in your folders, in the app's own folder on that device. A note leaves Spotlight when you delete it, hide its folder or remove its location. To leave Notebook's notes out of Spotlight, turn Notebook off in System Settings › Spotlight on the Mac, or in Notebook's search settings in Settings on iPhone and iPad.

On iPhone and iPad, deleting the app deletes everything it stored on the device, along with the notes kept in its own folder there. On the Mac, the app's preferences, its search index, its list of the notes in Spotlight and, from version 1.1, the versions of notes outside iCloud stay in its container, the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.lukaskbl.Notebook, until you delete that folder too. The folders you hide, your reminders and your Home and Work stay in iCloud's key-value store for your other devices, and, from version 1.1, the versions of notes in iCloud and the to-dos you added without a note in Notebook's iCloud folder, until you delete it in iCloud's storage settings. Your notes are your files and stay where they are.

Reminders, notifications and your location

  • Notifications of reminders and to-dos are scheduled on each of your devices by the system. Their text is the note's name or the to-do, the time or the place, and the note's first points or the to-do's notes, read from the note on that device. Notebook asks whether it may send notifications when you set your first reminder, and you can turn them off in the system's settings.
  • Your location. When you first give a reminder a place, Notebook asks whether it may use your location while you use the app ("While Using", never "Always"). On iPhone and iPad it hands the place to the system, which shows the notification when you arrive or leave; Notebook itself isn't told where you are and keeps no record of it. Current Location asks once where you are, to name the place, and keeps only the place you chose. A Mac doesn't watch places.

Camera and photos on iPhone and iPad

  • The camera is used only when you take a photo or scan a document into a note, and only after you have allowed it. The picture is saved next to the note, like any other.
  • Photos are chosen in the system's photo picker. Notebook receives only the pictures you pick and has no access to the rest of your library.

What the app sends, and to whom

  • Nothing of yours, with one exception you start: searching for a place for a reminder or a to-do asks Apple Maps, as the Maps app does, with the words you type; Current Location asks it for the address of where you are, an address you pick from Contacts for Home or Work asks it where that is, and the map in Remind Me loads from it. Notebook has no other code that connects to the internet. Pictures in notes that point to a web address are not downloaded; Notebook shows the address instead.
  • When you click or tap a web link in a note, your browser opens it.
  • If your notes are in iCloud Drive, Apple syncs them as it syncs every other file there. Notebook asks the system to download notes that aren't on your device yet, so it can show and search them. From version 1.1, Apple syncs the earlier versions of those notes, and the to-dos you added without a note, in Notebook's own iCloud folder, the same way. What Apple does with iCloud Drive is described in Apple's privacy policy.
